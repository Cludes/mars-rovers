JOURNEY TIMELINE
January 25, 2004 • Sol 1
Touchdown on Mars
Bounced to a landing inside tiny Eagle Crater. Engineers called it a "hole-in-one."
Sol 58
First evidence of ancient water
Found rock layers that could only have formed in liquid water. Mars had oceans!
Sol 95
Mission officially extended
Planned for 90 days. Was doing so well they just... kept going.
2007 • Sol 1,000+
Survived a global dust storm
A planet-wide storm blotted out the sun. Oppy went silent for weeks then woke up.
2010 • Sol 2,246
Reached Endeavour Crater
Drove 21 km to reach a huge ancient crater. Found the oldest rocks she ever studied.
March 24, 2015 • Sol 3,968
First Mars Marathon
Crossed 42.195 km total distance. The first marathon ever run on another planet.
June 10, 2018 • Sol 5,111
Last contact
A massive global dust storm covered her solar panels. She sent one last signal, then went quiet. NASA tried to wake her 835 times over 8 months.
February 13, 2019
Farewell, Oppy
Mission officially ended. NASA played "I'll Be Seeing You" as the last transmission to Mars. She is still there today, resting on the rim of Endeavour Crater.
FINAL TRANSMISSION • JUNE 10, 2018
“My battery is low
and it’s getting dark.”
- Opportunity Rover
Oppy had been in a dust storm for weeks. These were the last words she ever sent to Earth. She had survived everything Mars threw at her for 15 years.
WHERE IS SHE NOW?

Opportunity is still sitting on Mars right now, on the western rim of Endeavour Crater, in a valley called Perseverance Valley — named years before the rover.

Her solar panels are likely covered in dust. But she’s still there, exactly where she stopped.

18.4446°N  |  Perseverance Valley, Endeavour Crater
A NOTE FOR ALEXANDER

The rover that came after Oppy — Perseverance — was named by a 13-year-old boy called Alexander Mather, in a competition open to all American students.

He wrote: “We are a species of explorers, and we will meet many setbacks on the way to Mars. However, we can persevere.”

Oppy showed us how.

Five robots. One planet. Millions of discoveries.
